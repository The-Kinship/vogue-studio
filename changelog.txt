0.6.1: Turn the palm. Mouse wheel on a hand (or elbow) rolls the forearm, the way a real arm turns the palm: palm to mouth, palm out for a "stop" hand, palm up. About 90 degrees each way from palm-down, then it stops (a bit more in Flexible mode). Works with the elbow bent. Dragging is unchanged, so the roll can't happen by accident.
0.6.0: Detail mode for hands and feet. Select a hand or foot and press "Detail": the body circles are swapped for small circles on that hand's finger joints (or that foot's toes), read from the bones your character actually has (IVCS adds more). Finger knuckles curl toward the palm and stop (a little bend back allowed), the thumb base moves two ways, toes curl down or lift. Dragging a base knuckle curls the whole finger. "Back to the whole body" returns to the normal circles.
0.5.1: Arms (and legs and hands) now pick their two movements from where they point right now, so a hanging arm can swing forward and back (like an underhand throw) as well as flap out to the side; an arm held out raises/lowers and swings forward/back. Still no twisting. Renamed "Left/Right arm" to "Left/Right shoulder" (it moves the whole arm from the shoulder) and the old shoulder joint to "collarbone" (shrug, shown when Simple mode is off).
0.5.0: Whole body. A diamond handle at the character's feet: drag up/down to raise or lower them (sit, kneel, float), drag sideways to slide them along the floor, mouse wheel to turn them. Reset place button. Mouse wheel on any body part's circle moves it the way that's hard to drag from the current camera angle (5 degrees per click, still within its joint stops). Undo/Redo cover poses and whole-body moves.
0.4.0: Joint stops. Each movement has a natural range from the standing pose (knees bend back to about 150, elbows forward, arms up to about 150 and down to the body, legs kick forward 120 and back 40, wrists, feet, neck and head within comfortable limits) and stops there instead of spinning around. The window says "reached its limit" when you push against one. New Flexibility setting: Normal, Flexible (about a third more) or No limits. A part already past a stop can still be moved back.
0.3.1: Whole limbs move together. Brio keeps every joint in whole-body space, so moving one joint used to leave the parts below it behind (the stretching and distortion). Now moving a part carries everything below it, like a doll: arm takes the elbow, hand and fingers; elbow takes the forearm and hand; leg takes the knee and foot; neck and head take the face and hair.
0.3.0: Doll-style posing. Body parts are now limbs (Left arm, Left elbow, Left hand, Left leg, Left knee, Left foot, plus hips, chest, neck, head) and only move the way a doll's would: elbows and knees are single hinges, feet point and flex, arms, legs and hands swing two ways, and nothing twists along its own length. Drag up/down and the part moves up/down on screen; drag sideways and it moves sideways. The first direction you drag locks the movement until you let go. Movement directions come from the skeleton's built-in standing pose, not the camera.
0.2.0: Drag to bend! Press a joint's circle and drag around it: the body part turns to follow your mouse, like a paper doll, and Brio holds the pose. Undo, Redo and Reset pose buttons. Simple mode (on by default) shows only the main joints so the chest isn't crowded; names now show on hover and on the selected part (you can turn all names back on). Drag strength setting for finer bending.
0.1.1: Vogue Studio now stays visible in GPose (it was being hidden when entering GPose). Also includes the build fix.
0.1.0: First piece: big, easy-to-hit circles on your character's main joints in GPose (hips, back, chest, neck, head, shoulders, arms, hands, thighs, lower legs, feet), with names. Click a circle or a name in the list to select that body part. Settings: circle size, show names, hide circles. Status lines say in words if something's missing (not in GPose, Brio not answering). Dragging to move comes next.
